FAQ

Questions we often hear

An EIA studies how a planned project may affect the environment and recommends measures to avoid, reduce or manage those effects. It helps regulators decide whether and how a project should proceed.

Requirements depend on the type, size and location of a project and on national regulations. Factories, estates, hotels, filling stations, farms, roads and similar developments are common examples. We confirm what applies to your project.

An environmental audit checks an existing operation against its permits, regulations and standards, finds gaps, and sets out corrective actions to keep you compliant.

An ESIA covers the same environmental ground as an EIA and also assesses social effects, such as impacts on communities and livelihoods. Larger or financed projects often need it.

They give a clear spatial picture of your site and surroundings, showing land use, water bodies, sensitive areas and change over time, which supports better siting, assessment and monitoring.
